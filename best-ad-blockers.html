The 5 best ad blockers for Chrome in 2026

Manifest V3 reshaped this entire category. The blocker that topped every list for a decade no longer runs on Chrome at all. Here is what is left, what each one gave up, and which to pick for what.

⚠️

Disclosure — read this first: this ranking is published by AdBlo, and AdBlo is placed first. That is a conflict of interest and you should weigh it. We have written real cons for our own product and real pros for the others, and where a rival is the better choice we say which and why. If you would rather read a ranking with no stake in the outcome, that is a reasonable instinct — go and read one.

What changed, and why it matters

Chrome's Manifest V2 phase-out disabled the full uBlock Origin for ordinary users, and remaining Manifest V2 extensions are being removed from the Chrome Web Store. Blocking now runs through a declarative API: the browser matches rules natively, and the extension never sees the request. That is faster, and it is also less flexible — every blocker on this list is working within it.

1

AdBlo

Best for staying on Chrome

Built for Manifest V3 from the first line rather than cut down to fit it, which is why it kept the element picker and scriptlets that other V3 blockers dropped. Its distinguishing feature is not blocking but recovery: when a site breaks, it works out which filter layer did it. No acceptable ads, no telemetry, no account.

Pros

  • Only blocker that diagnoses site breakage automatically
  • Per-site pause with real timers and a countdown
  • Full element picker and 8 scriptlet primitives under Manifest V3
  • Stealth mode: camera, mic, location and WebRTC lockdown
  • No acceptable ads programme of any kind
  • Zero network code — nothing can phone home
  • Built-in health check tells you if a filter list failed to load

Cons

  • New in 2026 — no track record, small user base
  • Filter lists update with the extension, not on their own
  • Fewer regional and language-specific lists than mature rivals
  • Cannot beat full uBlock Origin on Firefox for raw power
  • Like every extension, useless against server-side video ads
2

uBlock Origin Lite

Best for minimalists

The Manifest V3 successor to the most respected blocker ever made, from the same author. Deliberately minimal: it does the declarative part extremely well and does not attempt the rest. Its own documentation is candid that it is less effective against anti-adblock techniques than its predecessor.

Pros

  • Impeccable provenance and filter curation
  • Extremely low memory and CPU footprint
  • Fully open source, no monetisation of any kind
  • Excellent default list selection

Cons

  • No element picker
  • No scriptlet injection
  • No dynamic filtering
  • Pause is on/off only, with no timer
  • Weaker against anti-adblock walls, by its author's own account
3

AdGuard

Best if you want the whole ecosystem

The most feature-complete of the established extensions, with a mature filter engine, its own well-maintained lists and a full element picker. The browser extension is free; the wider product line includes paid desktop and mobile applications that block system-wide.

Pros

  • Rich feature set, including a proper element picker
  • Excellent first-party filter lists, updated frequently
  • Strong regional and language list coverage
  • Desktop app blocks across every browser and app

Cons

  • Best experience is behind the paid desktop application
  • Settings are dense — a lot of surface for a casual user
  • No breakage diagnosis or timed pause
  • Heavier than the minimal options
4

Ghostery

Best for seeing who is tracking you

Comes at the problem from the tracker side rather than the advert side. Its real strength is legibility: it names the specific tracking companies on a page, which is genuinely educational in a way pure blockers are not.

Pros

  • Clearest picture of who is tracking you, by name
  • Strong anti-tracking heritage and open source core
  • Clean, approachable interface
  • No acceptable ads programme

Cons

  • Ad blocking is less thorough than the dedicated blockers
  • No element picker
  • Pushes a premium tier
  • No timed pause or breakage tooling
5

Adblock Plus

Best known, least strict

The extension that made ad blocking mainstream, and still one of the most installed. It is last here for one reason: its acceptable-ads whitelist is enabled by default and large advertisers pay to participate. You can switch it off — but a blocker that lets ads through until you find the setting is not what most people think they are installing.

Pros

  • Enormous install base and a long support history
  • Genuinely easy for non-technical users
  • Open source, free, no paid tier required
  • Acceptable ads can be disabled in one checkbox

Cons

  • Acceptable ads on by default — advertisers pay to be on the list
  • Less strict out of the box than everything above it
  • Limited element picker
  • No timed pause, no breakage diagnosis

Which should you actually install?

You are staying on Chrome

AdBlo or uBlock Origin Lite. Take AdBlo if you want the picker, timed pauses and breakage repair; take uBO Lite if you want the smallest, quietest thing that works.

You can change browser

Firefox with the full uBlock Origin, which remains more capable than anything Manifest V3 permits. We are not going to pretend otherwise to sell you our own extension.

You want blocking everywhere

AdGuard's desktop application, which filters outside the browser too — across other browsers and native apps. That is a category no extension can compete in.

You mainly care about trackers

Ghostery, for the visibility, ideally alongside a dedicated blocker. Knowing which companies are following you is worth something on its own.

Judge it yourself

Rankings are opinions. Install two, use them for a week, keep the one you forget about.
