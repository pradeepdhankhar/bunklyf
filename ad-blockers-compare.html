AdBlo vs the five blockers everyone else recommends

Chrome's move to Manifest V3 forced every blocker to give something up. This page sets out what each one lost, what it kept, and where AdBlo differs — including the places where a competitor is the better choice.

Where the differences actually bite

Acceptable ads

Adblock Plus and AdBlock both operate an acceptable-ads whitelist that is enabled by default, and large advertisers pay to participate in it. You can switch it off in the settings of both. AdBlo has no such programme — not off by default, but absent entirely.

What Manifest V3 took

uBlock Origin Lite is a deliberate reduction of uBlock Origin, and its own documentation says it is less effective against sites using anti-adblock techniques. AdBlo started from the Manifest V3 API rather than shrinking to fit it, which is why the picker and scriptlets survived.

The pause problem

Every blocker lets you disable itself on a site. Almost none let you say for how long. That is how people end up with a whitelist of forty sites they unblocked once in 2023. AdBlo's pauses expire on a timer and show a countdown.

When something breaks

The standard advice is to disable lists one at a time and reload until it works. AdBlo automates that as a binary search: nine possible culprits become about four questions, then a one-click fix. It is the feature we would miss most if we switched away.

When not to use AdBlo

If you can leave Chrome, leave Chrome

The full uBlock Origin on Firefox is more capable than any Manifest V3 blocker, AdBlo included. It can inspect each request individually with knowledge of the page that asked for it — something Chrome's declarative API does not hand back. If your only requirement is maximum blocking power and you are willing to change browser, that is the better answer.

AdBlo exists for people who are staying on Chrome. Within that constraint we think it is the best option. Outside it, we are not going to pretend.
